Paper record Digitized Validated

Most of India's land history still lives in ink.

Ownership, tenure, and mutation history are recorded by hand in registers kept at the tehsil office — the same registers a sale, a loan, or an inheritance claim depends on decades later.

Records damaged

Fire, flooding, and termites can erase decades of entries that were never copied anywhere else.

Records disputed

One mismatched survey number between the register and the map can freeze a sale for years.

Records duplicated

The same parcel, entered on two different pages, by two different clerks, decades apart.

Modules

Four systems, one record.

Each module addresses a different way a paper record breaks down — from illegible ink to a quietly altered entry.

Digitization engine

OCR & script recognition

Reads handwritten patta, khatauni, and mutation registers in regional scripts, and turns ink into structured fields: owner, parcel, area, and tenure.

AI validation layer

Consistency checks

Cross-checks every digitized entry against prior mutations and neighboring records, flagging mismatches before they harden into disputes.

GIS linkage

Spatial mapping

Ties every record to its exact surveyed boundary, so what the paper says and what the map shows finally agree.

Tamper-evident ledger

Chained records

Hashes every verified change into a chained record, so a parcel's history can be audited but never quietly rewritten.

Process

From ink to an entry nobody can quietly change.

A record moves through five stages before it's considered verified — each one narrowing the gap between what's on paper and what's true.

1

Scan & capture

Field teams digitize source registers village by village, preserving the original image alongside the extracted text.

2

Structure

OCR and language models extract owner, parcel, area, and mutation history into clean, searchable fields.

3

Validate

The system checks each entry against GIS boundaries, prior mutations, and adjoining records for inconsistencies.

4

Seal

A verified record is hashed and added to the chain — closing the door on edits made without a trace.

5

Publish

Citizens and revenue officers can view, and rely on, the verified record online — with its full history attached.

About

Part of a larger mandate.

The Ministry of Rural Development, through its Department of Land Resources, has long overseen the modernization of India's land records — moving them from tehsil registers toward a single, dependable digital source of truth.

Bhumi Setu is conceived as the validation layer within that effort: the step where a digitized record is checked, cross-referenced, and sealed before anyone downstream is asked to trust it.

Village-level captureDigitization organized by tehsil and village, not by document type alone
Cross-checked, not just scannedEvery entry validated against GIS boundaries and mutation history
